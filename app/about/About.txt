1. Who We Are
Rooted in the Department of Computer Science within the College of Science at Kwame
Nkrumah University of Science and Technology, the Computer Science Society (CSS) is the
active hub of student technology culture on campus. We represent more than 3,500 students
enrolled across both our Computer Science and Information Technology programmes.
CSS operates as an inclusive, student-led collective bringing together software engineers,
network architects, data analysts, security researchers, and builders at every stage of their
academic journey. Whether someone is writing their first program in an introductory lab or
deploying distributed cloud architectures late at night, our society exists to ensure they have
the community, academic backing, and practical opportunities needed to succeed.
2. Our Philosophy: Collaboration Over Competition
Technical education can be demanding, but our culture is anchored on a straightforward
principle: no member navigates the department alone.
● Open Exchange of Knowledge: We actively push back against gatekeeping.
Solutions to complex bugs, study materials, and practical design patterns are shared
openly through peer tutorials, group repositories, and community discussion boards.
● Continuous Mentorship: Senior cohorts actively invest in junior students. This
happens in campus study spaces and systematically through organized code
reviews, resume clinics, and career webinars featuring KNUST alumni working in
global tech.
● Curiosity with Direction: We encourage members to move beyond the boundaries
of lecture slides. If a student is curious about an emerging framework, a new
language, or an unaddressed local challenge, the society provides the peer network
to explore and build it.
3. Two Disciplines, One Community: CS and IT
Our department brings together two closely related, equally critical academic tracks. While
both share a foundation in computing principles and mathematics, they address distinct
challenges in the technology landscape.
BSc. Computer Science
Computer Science focuses on the mathematical foundations of computation, algorithmic
problem-solving, and large-scale software engineering. Students dive into how software
functions from the metal up.
● Core Areas of Study: Data Structures and Algorithms, Object-Oriented
Programming, Computer Architecture, Operating Systems, Theory of Computation,
Compiler Engineering, and Machine Learning.
● Where It Leads: Software Engineering, Systems Architecture, Machine Learning and
Data Science, Algorithmic Trading, Compiler Design, and Academic Research.
BSc. Information Technology
Information Technology focuses on designing, deploying, securing, and maintaining the
digital infrastructure that modern organizations rely upon. It emphasizes systems integration,
communication networks, and operational resilience.
● Core Areas of Study: Network Engineering and Protocols, Cloud Infrastructure
(AWS, Azure, GCP), Database Administration, Cybersecurity and Threat Mitigation,
Enterprise Systems Administration, and Web Technologies.
● Where It Leads: Cloud Solutions Architecture, DevOps Engineering, Information
Security and Penetration Testing, Network Engineering, and Enterprise Database
Administration.
4. Academic Leadership and Faculty
Our society works hand in hand with the lecturers and academic staff of the Department of
Computer Science.
The faculty members are researchers, industry advisors, and mentors who bring practical
experience into the lecture theatre. Their work spans artificial intelligence focused on local
challenges, applied cryptographic systems, data governance, and mobile computing.
Beyond delivering the formal curriculum, faculty members maintain open-door access during
office hours, support student-led research initiatives, and encourage departmental projects
that go on to compete in national and continental innovation challenges.

6. Navigating the Department: Essential Offices &
Where to Go
When administrative or academic questions arise, knowing exactly where to go saves time
and avoids frustration. Below is the operational map and detailed guide for key departmental
offices:
Head of Department (HOD) — Room FF4 (First Floor)
The Head of Department serves as the chief academic and administrative authority for the
Department of Computer Science, overseeing faculty operations, academic standards, and
high-level student governance.
● When to consult this office: Official introductory and recommendation letters for
internships, national service, or graduate school applications; formal requests for
programme transfers, deferments, or leave of absence; final appeal channel for
academic and administrative issues; endorsement of official student club or society
activities; and serious student welfare or disciplinary matters.
Department Examinations Officer — Room FF14 (First Floor)
The Examinations Officer coordinates mid-semester tests, end-of-semester examinations,
assessment records, and official academic performance evaluations for all undergraduate
cohorts.
● When to consult this office: Missing mid-semester or continuous assessment (CA)
marks on your portal; timetable clashes during mid-semester or final examination
periods; official petitions for paper re-marking; registration inquiries for deferred,
supplementary, or trail examinations; and verification or correction of semester grade
entries.
General Department Secretary — Room FF4 (First Floor Secretariat)
The General Secretary’s office functions as the primary administrative hub and front-desk
reception for all general student and visitor inquiries.
● When to consult this office: Submitting official letters, petitions, or forms intended
for the HOD; picking up approved recommendation or introductory letters; general
inquiries about departmental notices, events, and schedules; processing student
clearance forms; verifying departmental course registration; and booking
appointments with the HOD.
CSS Office - FF2
The administrative home of your elected student leaders.
● When to consult this office: Enquiries about peer-led subject clinics, hackathon
team registrations, society dues and financial receipts, official society merchandise,
and suggestions for student welfare.
Quick Reference: "Where Should I Go?" Guide
● Introductory/Recommendation Letter: Request at the General Secretary (FF4) →
Signed by HOD (FF4).
● Missing Exam / Assessment Marks: First report to Course Lecturer → Escalated to
Exams Officer (FF14).
● Exam Timetable Clash: Department Examinations Officer (FF14) immediately upon
release of the timetable.
● Programme Deferment / Transfer: Formal letter addressed to HOD, submitted
through General Secretary (FF4).
● Peer Support & Tutorials: CSS Office (FF2) or reach out to executives.
● Society Dues & Receipts: Financial Secretary at the CSS Office (FF2).
7. Beyond the Lecture Hall: Technical Initiatives
We believe technical confidence comes from applying classroom theory to working projects
before graduation.
● Subject Clinics and Peer Tutorials: Weekly peer-guided revision sessions
organized ahead of mid-semester and final exams, tackling foundational math,
programming, and system modules.
● Code Quest and Code Fest: Our flagship 24-to-48-hour development sprints and
hackathons where student teams build working solutions for practical local problems.
● Technical Workshops and Bootcamps: Hands-on weekend masterclasses
covering modern tools and frameworks not always detailed in standard curricula,
including full-stack development, mobile development with Flutter, containerization
with Docker, and foundational cybersecurity.
● CSS Media Network: Student-run media outlets, including the CSS official YouTube
channel, spotlighting student projects and journeys, and department events.
8. Sports and Camaraderie: The CSS Games
A balanced engineer is a better engineer. Physical competition and inter-year sports remain
central to our community culture, offering an outlet from terminal windows and tedious labs.
The annual CSS Games bring together all cohorts in a structured tournament spanning
football, basketball, volleyball, athletics, and indoor games.
